# Data-use norms

The data on this site is open. The licence requires attribution. It cannot require honesty. These norms are what we ask of anyone who uses it, and what we will say publicly when they are not met.

1. **Cite the view, not the number.** Every chart has a permalink and a "cite this view" card that carries the date range, the sample size, the confidence band, and the caveat. Quote the card.
2. **Do not separate a measure from its counter-measure.** Vehicles per hour without people per hour, or collision counts without exposure, is a misreading of the site.
3. **Do not describe a raw comparison as a finding.** Views labelled raw are not weather-, season-, or weekday-matched.
4. **Do not infer cause from a timeline.** Only registered difference-in-differences views make a comparative claim, and even those list their confounders.
5. **Say what is missing.** If a segment or period is greyed out, say so.
6. **Attribute.** StreetLens, the snapshot date, and the original publishers listed in the source catalogue.

## What happens when these are not met

The project keeps a public "Cited in" page. Uses that meet the norms are listed. Uses that do not are listed with a short, factual note about what the citation left out. We do not characterize motive. We do not remove the citation.

## What the project asks of itself

- Publish the analysis plan before the results.
- Publish the methodology, the code, the funding sources, and every correction.
- Keep a methodology advisory group whose members hold different views on the underlying policy question, and publish their names and affiliations.
- Never publish an interpretation without a named author and a version.
